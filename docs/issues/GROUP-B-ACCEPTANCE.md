## Follow-up audit on 2026-10-08

Signal-gated checks on the previously published `342aeb7` runtime reproduced two endpoint-callback observations: delayed work could replace a newer Pair, or restart playback after Stop. Their combined desired assertion failed. Four separate Core assertions also failed when Pair or Stop arrived during retarget cleanup or settings-restart cleanup. These follow-up results are separate from the seven observations on original `41190e0` recorded below. The endpoint/retarget comparison kept the relevant published production files unchanged; the settings comparison verified the unchanged production method bodies.

Follow-up commit `11c60466465bc07c0d6ce519dcfc4d3a54b973f6` changes four files. The [application callback](https://github.com/ThatMarko/AirFlash/blob/11c60466465bc07c0d6ce519dcfc4d3a54b973f6/desktop/AirFlash.App/ViewModels/AppViewModel.cs) captures ownership and intent before awaiting endpoint enumeration, and the [controller](https://github.com/ThatMarko/AirFlash/blob/11c60466465bc07c0d6ce519dcfc4d3a54b973f6/desktop/AirFlash.Core/SessionController.cs) validates both under serialization. Retarget/settings restarts recheck intent after cleanup. If cleanup ends an active worker and a rejected replacement leaves no successor, the snapshot becomes Idle; an already inactive Error snapshot and its diagnostics remain intact.

Endpoint restoration remains serialized with startup. A notification received while the worker is finishing is coalesced into one deferred consumer plus the latest pending worker/owner/intent tuple. Waiting for that worker holds neither controller serialization nor the application settings gate. Recovery rechecks ownership, intent, inactive state and disposal before touching audio; obsolete requests cannot restore a replacement's mute state. Tests cover live worker retry, replacement, rejected explicit intent, queued/admitted recovery during disposal, both prior mute bits, and delivery of deferred failures to the application notice path.
